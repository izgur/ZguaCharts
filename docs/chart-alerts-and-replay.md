# Chart alerts and replay

Alerts are local notifications, not trading orders. The Flask server must remain running. Rules and recent notification events are stored in data/chart-alerts.json; the server checks active rules every 30 seconds. Price uses the latest observed close. Indicators use completed candles, the existing backend formulas, and their default parameters. No historical notifications are generated when creating a rule. Multiple conditions support all/any matching; repeated level alerts rearm after becoming false. Crossing alerts require a prior observation. Expired and once-triggered rules stop automatically.

Browser toasts/sound require an open app tab. Email works without an open browser, but requires local SMTP settings. Create config/local/alert-email.json with host, port, security (starttls or ssl), from, to, username and password. Alternatively use ZGUA_SMTP_HOST, ZGUA_SMTP_PORT, ZGUA_SMTP_SECURITY, ZGUA_SMTP_FROM, ZGUA_SMTP_TO, ZGUA_SMTP_USERNAME and ZGUA_SMTP_PASSWORD. Do not commit credentials. Email remains disabled until host/from/to are configured. Delivery failures appear in saved alerts and notification events; no mail was sent during implementation tests.

Replay affects only the selected chart. Choose a starting candle; that candle and subsequent candles are initially hidden. Step/play reveals one new bar at a time. Live feeds, history pagination and strategy refresh are paused. Indicators are recomputed on the visible prefix only; existing strategy markers, overlays, drawings and live signal details are suppressed to avoid leaking future results. Replay is visual bar playback, not an intrabar execution simulator or a strategy backtest. Exit reloads the live chart. Changing market/timeframe also exits replay. Other charts and paper/research processes continue normally.

The 6m/12m chart intervals aggregate native 3m candles (or 1m where needed), UTC-aligned. Missing historical buckets are discarded, not fabricated. Current partial buckets may be shown. These intervals use REST polling rather than an unsupported exchange WebSocket subscription. History depth remains constrained by the underlying provider; they do not provide extra historical data.
